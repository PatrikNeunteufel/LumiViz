2b Vibro-acoustical signals

A light-weight impulse hammer (9 grams) is used to induce mechanical impulses at three
alternative positions at the bridge. The positions are located at the inlay of the bridge to
represent the application of string-enforced excitement. The positions are labelled

treble: between strings si and mi, B3 and E4
central: between strings re and sol, D3 and G3
bass: between strings mi and la, E2 and A2

Two signals are taken at the bridge with the help
of acceleration sensors (1 gram). The two signals
at the bass and the treble side taken together
allow to trace two of the degrees of freedom of
the bridge.

                                                       Fig. 3: Positions of hammer impact and sensors.

2c Near-field microphones

Three sound pressure microphones are used to capture
near-field radiation 10 cm above the top plate.
Two microphones are located above the bridge,
20 cm apart, and another one at the upper part of the guitar top,
again 20 cm away from the treble side microphone, where
flamenco guitarists usually place their stage microphone. This
minimum set is very sparse and does not allow near-field
reconstruction, but it can serve to estimate radiation efficiency.

The microphone positions are labelled (in MATLAB files):

treble: 10 cm from the bridge center to the treble side
bass: 10 cm from the bridge center to the bass side
upper east: 20 cm away from the treble position

all of them 10 cm above the surface of the top plate.

2d Far-field microphones

The sound field is also captured at the beginning of the far-field, at 1 m above the top plate,
centrally between the sound hole and the bridge. The related data is not part of this data set.
The data is used to reference to standard sound pressure measurements.

The total flow of capturing, normalization, and processing to obtain vibro-acoustical and near-
field transfer-functions is as shown in the diagramm.

                                   Fig. 6: Signal capturing, normalization and processing.

The total size of raw data is 3 Gbyte, holding 3 to 12 samples of each impulse position, times
three impact positions, times 6 channels, sampled at 48 kHz.
The raw data holds enough good examples to do statistics.
But for the purpose of documentation, only one good example for each impulse position is
selected and cut to a length of 1 second, yielding a resolution of 1 Hz in the transfer functions.
There are two selection criteria: avoiding the `second impulse', and minimum impact force.
In rare cases, the induced hammer impulse is followed by a secondary impulse caused by the
refleting impulses in the device under test. The qualification procedure rejects samples with
such secondary impulses, if they have a level of more than -30dB with reference to the
intended impact impulse. The only exception is for guitar g05, where the level reaches -22.5
dB and -27.5 dB, for the bass and treble side, respectively.
Another criterion of selection is the minimum impact force. Only impulses with more than 10
Newton force are taken. There are few exceptions to this, as this criterion has not been
checked during instrumentation: g04 worked with 8.6 N on the bass side impact, g24 with 5 N
on the treble side impact, and g55 with 7,8 N on the bass side impact.
This publication finally holds the `qualified but not yet normalized' selected raw data,
31 Mbyte. The related MATLAB files contain the steps of normalization and reference to
physical SI units, while respecting the data sheets and calibration issues for the sensors.

4 Data organisation

The MATLAB file qualified_selected_impulses.mat contains the matrix labelled:
qualified_selected_impulses, which is organized as follows

qualified_selected_impulses(guitar_number, signal, channel)
guitar number 1:65, see reference list in the documentation
signal 1:144000, 3 x 1 second of sound @ 48 ksamples, bass/center/treble
channels:
1 = impulse hammer
2 = sensor treble side
3 = sensor bass side
4 = mic upper east
5 = mic treble side
6 = mic bass side
